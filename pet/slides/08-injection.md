<p class="sub">FDG travels with the blood and reaches every organ, including the brain</p>
<p class="takeaway">The brain treats FDG like ordinary glucose.</p>

Note:
A small dose of FDG is injected into a vein. The blood carries it everywhere; cells
that need glucose start pulling it in.

The patient then rests for roughly 30 to 60 minutes before scanning, so that the
tracer has time to accumulate. For a brain scan this is done in a quiet, dimly lit
room with eyes open and no talking or reading, because brain activity changes glucose
use — whatever the brain is doing during uptake is what the image will show.
Patients also fast beforehand, since high blood sugar competes with FDG.

Then the head is placed in the detector ring and the scan begins.
