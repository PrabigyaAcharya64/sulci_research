<p class="sub">voxel (i, j, k) in the PET image and voxel (i, j, k) in the MRI are not the same point in the head</p>

Note:
The PET and the MRI of the same person are two separate 3D grids. A structural T1 MRI
typically has voxels of about 1 mm on each side and many of them (for example
256 × 256 × 176). PET voxels are larger — commonly 2 to 4 mm — so the same head is
covered by far fewer voxels. The two images also have different fields of view,
different origins, and the head was positioned differently in each scanner, so the
grids are tilted relative to each other.

The consequence: you cannot take voxel (i, j, k) in one image and look up voxel
(i, j, k) in the other. To relate them, both grids are first tied to physical
coordinates in millimetres — next slide.
