<div class="eq sm" data-tex="\mathbf p_{\text{PET}}=T^{-1}\,\mathbf v=R^{T}(\mathbf v-\mathbf t)\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad \mathrm{SUV}(\mathbf v)=\text{interpolate PET at }\mathbf p_{\text{PET}}"></div>
<p class="sub">T maps PET → MRI, so its inverse takes a mesh vertex back into the PET image</p>

Note:
The registration gave us T, which maps a PET point into MRI space (x′ = Rx + t). The
mesh vertex v already lives in MRI space, so we go the other way with the inverse:
p = T⁻¹v = Rᵀ(v − t). For a rotation the inverse is just the transpose, so nothing
needs to be solved — it is a direct formula.

p is a position in millimetres in the PET image. The PET voxel-to-millimetre matrix
(from the image header) converts it into a fractional voxel index, which almost never
falls exactly on a voxel centre. The SUV at the vertex is therefore interpolated from
the surrounding voxels — trilinear interpolation uses the 8 nearest voxel centres in
3D (4 are drawn here in 2D), weighted by distance.

Practical detail: FreeSurfer's surface coordinates use its own "surface RAS"
convention, so in practice tools such as mri_vol2surf (with a registration from
bbregister or mri_coreg) handle that coordinate bookkeeping. To limit partial-volume
blurring, PET is usually sampled midway between the white and pial surfaces (the
middle of the cortical ribbon) rather than at either boundary.
