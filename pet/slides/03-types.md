Note:
Brain PET. FDG measures glucose metabolism, which tracks neuronal activity; different
dementias reduce it in characteristic regions, and in epilepsy the seizure focus is
often hypometabolic between seizures. Amyloid PET binds the beta-amyloid plaques of
Alzheimer's disease; tau PET binds the neurofibrillary tangles.

Cancer PET. Many tumours consume far more glucose than normal tissue (the Warburg
effect), so FDG makes them light up. It is used to detect tumours, to stage them —
how far they have spread — and to see whether treatment is working. Other cancer
tracers exist, for example PSMA tracers for prostate cancer.

The physics and the scanner are identical in both cases. Only the tracer changes,
and that is the point of the next slides.
