<h2>What Happens at One Vertex</h2>
<div class="eq sm lead" data-tex="\text{(1)}\quad c_{u_1,i}=c_{u_1}(\theta_i,\phi_i),\quad c_{u_2,i}=c_{u_2}(\theta_i,\phi_i),\quad \omega_i=\omega(\theta_i,\phi_i)"></div>
<div class="eq sm" data-tex="\text{(2)}\quad z_{T,i}=c_{u_1,i}u_1+c_{u_2,i}u_2\quad\longrightarrow\quad \bar z_i=\exp_z(z_{T,i})"></div>
<div class="eq sm" data-tex="\text{(3)}\quad R_i=R_{2,i}\,R_{1,i}\quad\longrightarrow\quad M(p_i)=R_i\,p_i"></div>
<p class="takeaway">The same three steps as before — now evaluated separately at every vertex.</p>

Note:
Walk it through out loud. Take a vertex. Evaluate the three harmonic functions at
that vertex's own coordinates, which gives three numbers belonging to this vertex
alone. The first two build a tangent vector, and the exponential map turns it into
this vertex's target axis. Going from the reference axis to that one is the
precession; the third number supplies the intrinsic spin. Multiply the two
matrices and apply the result to the vertex. Nothing new has been introduced since
the composite-rotation slide — the only change is that the three numbers are now
read off a smooth field instead of being fixed constants.
